# The Big "Why Ruby?" Update: New Domain, New Features, Fresh Design

A few weeks ago, something happened that made me rethink everything about WhyRuby.info.

The official Ruby website, ruby-lang.org, added a "Why Ruby?" section right on their home page. And I sat there thinking: wait, isn't that exactly what my site was supposed to do?

Vladimir Dementiev suggested something that changed my direction: why not let all developers share why they love Ruby? Not just the famous quotes, but everyone's story.

So now you can. On your profile, write a couple of sentences about why you love Ruby. The AI takes your words and turns them into a testimonial with a heading, a subheading, and a bit of expansion on your idea. The carousel on the home page rotates through all of them. Each one links back to your profile.

The AI part was tricky. I spent time making sure it doesn't sound like AI. No "tapestry of innovation" or "testament to excellence." Just your voice, slightly polished. If the system detects its own output sounds robotic, it regenerates. Up to three times.

## New features

There was a list of feature requests sitting in my notes. Here are the ones that made it:

**Hide repositories.** Click the eye icon on any repo in your profile, and it disappears from the public view. For that experimental project you'd rather not showcase.

**"Open to Work" badge.** Toggle it in your profile settings, and a red badge appears on your avatar.

**Bio links.** Mention @someone in your bio, and it links to their GitHub. Drop a URL, and it becomes clickable.

The design got a refresh too. Ruby-shaped avatars instead of circles. Updated favicon. The whole site feels cleaner.

## Ruby Community gets its own domain

The Community page became surprisingly popular. People were sorting by stars, filtering by company and location, discovering each other's open source projects.

It deserved its own domain: **rubycommunity.org**.

Same app underneath. Same database, same users. Sign in on one site, and you're signed in on the other. Sign out on one, sign out on both. But now the community has its own identity. A place about Ruby developers themselves.

When you visit rubycommunity.org, you land on the community grid. Clean URLs: rubycommunity.org/yourusername. When you visit whyruby.info, you get the content side, success stories, and the testimonial carousel.
